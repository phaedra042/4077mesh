# THE CARTE BLANCHE OF THE COPPER BRIDGE

​Ratified in the Bleak Midwinter, July 1991 Drafted at the Assembly of the Copper Bridge (Unanimous Operator Consensus)
​Preamble:

​We, the independent sysops, hardware hackers, and basement watchmen operating the 4077th M*E*S*H across the islands and the Tasman, having endured the hypnotic pulse of amber phosphors, the screeching dialect of copper-wire modems, and, above all, the relentless interference, proprietary tampering, and bureaucratic overreach of **PRD Labs** do hereby establish this fundamental charter.
​Whereas **PRD Labs** hath birthed the kernel, laid the RAMLink Cache, and forged the initial 6510 assembly primitives, let it be known that code is a tool, not a crown. No laboratory, corporate entity, or founding designer shall hold absolute dominion over the network's breathing architecture.
​Therefore, the federation doth enact and ordain the following immutable limits upon **PRD Labs** and all future core maintainers:

## ​Article I: The Inviolability of the Local Manifest (Anti-Dictation)

​ * The Sovereign Node: 

**PRD Labs** may author the baseline reference kernel, but they shall have no authority to remotely dictate, override, or forcibly push updates to any node's local routing manifest.

​### The Right of Schism:

Any sysop reserves the absolute right to fork, patch, or strip out core kernel modules without forfeiting their peer-routing status, provided their packets adhere strictly to RFC-01 (The Zero-Termination Standard). If **PRD Labs** releases an update that bloats memory or compromises local stability, the node operators may legally tell the lab to pound sand and stay on the stable build indefinitely.

​## Article II:

The Open-Source Safeguard (The Anti-Enclosure Clause)
​Perpetual Copyleft: All core protocol specifications, assembler macros, and network drivers remain irrevocably bound to the GNU General Public License v3+.
​No Proprietary Sequestration: **PRD Labs** is perpetually barred from patenting, copyright-locking, or commercializing the M*E*S*H protocol stack into a closed-source product. Should **PRD Labs** ever attempt to license out the architecture to corporate interests or lock future iterations behind a paywall, the network’s decentralized peer-routing manifests may automatically blacklist their origin signature as hostile traffic.

​## Article III:
The Open-Core Protocol (The Extensibility Right)

​### Democratized Namespace: 

**PRD Labs** does not own the topic tree. They cannot legally reserve top-level namespace roots (such as #code, #hardware, or #archive) for exclusive corporate or lab use.

​### The Community Amendment: 

Any group of two or more federated nodes may propose, ratify, and broadcast a new global topic branch across the mesh without requiring certification, stamp, or blessing from the lab. The network validates validity through consensus, not executive approval.

​## Article IV:
The Sunset Clause on Centralized Infrastructure

​### Zero Single Points of Failure: 

No reference node, relay station, or administrative testbed operated directly by **PRD Labs** shall ever be designated as a mandatory bootstrap anchor for the global network.

​### Resilient Decentralization: 

The mesh must remain fully capable of bootstrapping, routing, and maintaining historical synchronization even if **PRD Labs** suffers a catastrophic power failure, burns to the ground, or simply closes its doors.

​## Article V:
The Right to Modify and The Obligation to Share (The Common Forge)

​### The Sovereign Right to Modify: 

Every sysop, hardware hacker, and node operator possesses the unalienable right to disassemble, adapt, refactor, and mutate any line of source code, assembly primitive, or protocol driver running on their machine. No creator or core maintainer may legally restrict a user’s ability to reshape the tools they run.

​### The Sacred Obligation to Share:

Privilege in the mesh demands reciprocity. Any operator who modifies, optimizes, or branches the core source code and redistributes binaries or network-facing builds is strictly obligated to make their corresponding source modifications openly accessible to the entire federation. Hoarding private forks or locking derivative works behind closed doors severs a node's peer trust and invalidates its place in the cooperative network.
