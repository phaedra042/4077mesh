# THE CARTE BLANCHE OF DUNEDIN
Ratified in the Bleak Midwinter, July 1991Drafted at the Assembly of the Copper Bridge (Unanimous Operator Consensus)

## Preamble:
We, the independent sysops, hardware hackers, and basement watchmen operating the 4077th ```M*E*S*H``` across the islands and the Tasman, having endured the flickering phosphors, the screech of analog modems, and the constant threat of hardware obsolescence, do hereby establish this fundamental charter.*

Whereas PRD Labs hath birthed the kernel, laid the RAMLink Write-Ahead Logs, and forged the initial 6510 assembly primitives, let it be known that code is a tool, not a crown. No laboratory, corporate entity, or founding designer shall hold absolute dominion over the network's breathing architecture.

Therefore, the federation doth enact and ordain the following immutable limits upon PRD Labs and all future core maintainers:
Article I: The Inviolability of the Local Manifest (Anti-Dictation)

    The Sovereign Node: PRD Labs may author the baseline reference kernel, but they shall have no authority to remotely dictate, override, or forcibly push updates to any node's local routing manifest (manifest.cfg).

    The Right of Schism: Any sysop reserves the absolute right to fork, patch, or strip out core kernel modules without forfeiting their peer-routing status, provided their packets adhere strictly to RFC-01 (The Zero-Termination Standard). If PRD Labs releases an update that bloats memory or compromises local stability, the node operators may legally tell the lab to pound sand and stay on the stable build indefinitely.

Article II: The Open-Source Safeguard (The Anti-Enclosure Clause)

    Perpetual Copyleft: All core protocol specifications, assembler macros, and network drivers remain irrevocably bound to the GNU General Public License v3+.

    No Proprietary Sequestration: PRD Labs is perpetually barred from patenting, copyright-locking, or commercializing the M*ESH protocol stack into a closed-source product. Should PRD Labs ever attempt to license out the architecture to corporate interests or lock future iterations behind a paywall, the network’s decentralized DNS and peer-routing manifests shall automatically blacklist their origin signature as hostile traffic.

Article III: The Open-Core Protocol (The Extensibility Right)

    Democratized Namespace: PRD Labs does not own the topic tree. They cannot legally reserve top-level namespace roots (such as #code, #hardware, or #archive) for exclusive corporate or lab use.

    The Community Amendment: Any group of three or more federated nodes may propose, ratify, and broadcast a new global topic branch across the mesh without requiring certification, stamp, or blessing from the lab. The network validates validity through consensus and RAMLink caching, not executive approval.

Article IV: The Sunset Clause on Centralized Infrastructure

    Zero Single Points of Failure: No reference node, relay station, or administrative testbed operated directly by PRD Labs shall ever be designated as a mandatory bootstrap anchor for the global network.

    Resilient Decentralization: The mesh must remain fully capable of bootstrapping, routing, and maintaining historical synchronization even if PRD Labs suffers a catastrophic power failure, burns to the ground, or simply closes its doors.

Signed and Sealed in the Copper Wire:

    The 8th Bit (Dunedin)

    The Iron Bastion (Christchurch)

    The Clockwork Grotto (Wellington)

    The Copper Wire Syndicate (Auckland)

    And all valid peer nodes holding active RAMLink manifests.
