# The Concordat of the Copper Bridge (1991)

The independent sysops and hardware hackers running the 4077th ```M*E*S*H``` across the Tasman have reached a consensus: **PRD Labs** built the core kernel, the RAMLink cache, and the 6510 assembly primitives, but they don't own the network.

Code is a tool, not a crown. To prevent further corporate overreach, proprietary tampering, and bureaucratic bloat, the federation establishes these immutable limits for all core maintainers:

## ​Article I:
The Inviolability of the Local Manifest (Anti-Dictation)
​
### The Sovereign Node:

**PRD Labs** may author the baseline reference kernel, but they shall have no authority to remotely dictate, override, or forcibly push updates to any node's local routing manifest.

### The Right of Schism:

Any sysop reserves the absolute right to fork, patch, or strip out core kernel modules without forfeiting their peer-routing status, provided their packets adhere strictly to RFC-01 (Formalities of the Handshake and Data Packet). If **PRD Labs** releases an update that bloats memory or compromises local stability, the node operators may under the concordat tell **PRD Labs** to pound sand and stay on the stable build indefinitely.

## Article II:
The Open-Source Safeguard (The Anti-Enclosure Clause)

### Perpetual Copyleft:
All core protocol specifications, assembler macros, and network drivers have been moved to and remain irrevocably bound to the GNU General Public License v3+.
​
### No Proprietary Sequestration:
**PRD Labs** is perpetually barred from patenting, copyright-locking, or commercializing the``M*E*S*H``protocol stack into a closed-source product. Should **PRD Labs** ever attempt to license out the architecture to corporate interests or lock future iterations behind a paywall, Individual nodes may elect to reject, filter, or quarantine traffic and treat their origin signature as hostile traffic.

## Article III:
The Open Architecture Protocol (The Extensibility Right)

### Democratized Namespace: 

**PRD Labs** does not own the topic tree. They shall not reserve top-level namespace roots (such as #code, #hardware, or #archive) for exclusive corporate or lab use.

### The Community Amendment: 

Any group of two or more federated nodes may propose, ratify, and broadcast a new global topic branch across the mesh without requiring certification, stamp, or blessing from the **PRD Labs**. The network establishes legitimacy through consensus, not executive approval..

## Article IV:
The Sunset Clause on Centralized Infrastructure

### Zero Single Points of Failure: 

No reference node, relay station, or administrative testbed operated directly by **PRD Labs** shall ever be designated as a mandatory bootstrap or anchor for the global network.

### Resilient Decentralization: 

The mesh must remain fully capable of bootstrapping, routing, and maintaining historical synchronization even if **PRD Labs** suffers a catastrophic power failure, burns to the ground, or simply closes its doors.

## Article V:
The Right to Modify and The Obligation to Share (The Common Forge)

### The Sovereign Right to Modify: 

Every sysop, hardware hacker, and node operator possesses the unalienable right to disassemble, adapt, refactor, and mutate any line of source code, assembly primitive, or protocol driver running on their machine. No creator or core maintainer may legally restrict a user’s ability to reshape the tools they run.

### The Sacred Obligation to Share:

Privilege in the mesh demands reciprocity. Any operator who modifies, optimizes, or branches the core source code and redistributes binaries or network-facing builds is strictly obligated to make their corresponding source modifications openly accessible to the entire federation. Hoarding private forks or locking derivative works behind closed doors severs a node's peer trust and invalidates its place in the cooperative network.
